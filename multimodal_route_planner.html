📚 Key Concepts: Multimodal vs Intermodal Transport

Multimodal Transport
Uses multiple modes under a single contract with one Multimodal Transport Operator (MTO). Door-to-door service with single liability.
Intermodal Transport
Uses multiple modes with separate contracts for each leg. Shipper coordinates and bears responsibility for each segment.
Transmodal Transport
Movement within the same mode (e.g., ship-to-ship transfer). Often used at transhipment hubs.

📋 SWOT Analysis: Road + Ferry + Road

💪 Strengths

  • Direct delivery flexibility
  • Good balance of cost and speed
  • Established ferry services

⚠️ Weaknesses

  • Dependent on ferry schedules
  • Possible delays at ports
  • Weather dependency

🚀 Opportunities

  • Consolidate shipments for better rates
  • Use off-peak ferry slots
  • Effective planning reduces delays

⚡ Threats

  • Customs and border issues
  • Fuel price fluctuations
  • Traffic congestion

💡 Trade-off Analysis & Decision Support

Current scenario: For general goods transport from Maidstone to Amiens, the Road-Ferry-Road combination offers the best balance. While direct road transport is faster, the ferry crossing reduces overall costs and provides a break for drivers (compliance with EU driving regulations).

Recommendation: If reducing carbon footprint is a priority, consider the rail option despite the longer transit time. Rail freight emits approximately 75% less CO₂ per tonne-km compared to road transport.
