📚 Key Theory: Modal Split & Utility

Modal Split is the percentage distribution of travellers using different transport modes. Mode Choice is influenced by utility — the satisfaction or benefit a traveller gains from choosing a particular mode. Factors include cost, time, comfort, reliability, and environmental awareness. Understanding these relationships is crucial for transport planning and achieving sustainable mobility goals.

👤 Traveller Characteristics

Higher income → more car ownership, less bus usage
Short trips favour walking/cycling; long trips favour rail/car
UK average: 1.2 cars per household

🌍 External Factors

Higher fuel prices → modal shift to public transport
Frequency, reliability, comfort of buses/trains
Dedicated lanes, safety, bike parking
High congestion → discourages car use
Higher awareness → preference for sustainable modes

📐 The Utility Function

Generalised Cost of Travel
C = c + (VOT × t) + θ

Where: c = monetary cost, VOT = value of time, t = travel time, θ = other factors (comfort, reliability, environment). Mode with lowest generalised cost has highest utility.
